# Beautiful day (I am writing in my own words - there is hope, there is hope)

*On the difference between knowing what matters and living it.*

Growing up, I was not lucid about the meaning of words. I could be lost in a song or a saying we used and never ask for its real meaning. I can use a word or even whole sentences for years as if they were idioms: I know what it means from the context, the atmosphere around it, and where people use it.

This happens sometimes today too. English is not my native language. I could parrot something for years, semi-sensing it, never asking what it is - having false confidence that I know by the context, vibes, etc. Then I dwell on it—“What does it actually mean?”—dissect it, look for its roots, or deliberately translate it into my native Macedonian, and then, in a flash, it hits me. There is a whole world behind some words.

In the family and culture I grew up in, this was regular; looking back, we are relatively much less deliberate and careful about the use of words. With the exception of the Orthodox Christian church order. Reading them gives me this pleasure in my own language. Ohhhh, words have meaning. There is care and intention in how they coin and use them.

Мило-срдие. The original intention of the church fathers, according to GPT, is basically mercy/compassion: “the heart becoming responsive to another being’s suffering, in a way that inclines toward mercy, help, forgiveness, or care.”  “Мило” can mean “being glad” and can be used toward a “beloved” child or a loved one today, but its deeper meaning in the context of милосрдие is mercy, loving-kindness, compassion. “Срд” in the original coinage was meant as “heart”, so mercy with the heart in it. Although there is a modern word “Срдечност,” which is sincerity in my view. So I see in it Sincere Care in one beautiful harmonious word.

There is a reason why prayer is key in Orthodoxy - it lets you dwell on this until words and what they refer to become illuminated. “Благо-слов” literally means sweet word; more deeply, it means good word - a blessing, which I connect to well-wishing, loving-kindness practice, intentionally wishing someone good. “Добро-љубие” literally means good-love; it was intended as the love of the good - a playful fork from the love of wisdom. In добро-љубие I hear a whole devotional orientation, their system prompt if you like, in a coinage of two words beautifully put together in our Slavic languages. They have one word on their second most important book after the Bible, a single word: Доброљубие/Philokalia. Talk to me about compression! Really no need to open that book. Getting to the heart of the matter in a way that could stick as both a devotional practice north star and a moral/virtue-ethics/value/aesthetic kernel. I even felt it as identity - since they mean it as a noun, but I feel it more as a verb - active direction/inclination/calling,  a process of unfolding-activity, but most basically - as a deeply felt sense/realization of the rightness/intinsic worth/goodness/beauty of the Good.

And there is “гони-ветар” - a relatively obscure word - translation of Ecclesiastes’s “chasing after the wind” in a single elegant folkish coinage. Later, I guess, that one influenced the much less respectable “еби-ветар”, “fucking the wind” or as it is today, meant “wind-fucker,” used for someone you shouldn’t take seriously, sometimes even used half-jokingly about me by some of my less kind friends. Good, I take that too; as a child I was deeply hurt when the same “friends” called me all kinds of names; I paradoxically aspire to be the holy fool, so far just a fool. I picture Don Quixote, lost in his own story, fighting windmills, out of touch with (conventional) reality. But follow the association back to Ecclesiastes and the joke includes the concerns of conventional reality too. Work, possessions, all that serious business of holding it all together, getting somewhere: chasing the wind. Little do the people laughing at the wind-fucker know about this.

Early in my contemplative efforts, I once got so high that some weird shit happened where I was at the same time the substrate, reality itself, the projector and the screen on which the movie is projected, and the character; I was there lucid in the middle of my own Don Quixote story, more accurately the so sweet, and significant to me - Petre Laskov story. Regular day, in bed, with my wife trying to spice it up a little. That kind of contrast has not happened to me even on month-long retreats. Here are the projector and the screen and the movie on the screen and the character in the movie - Reality, Nature of Mind itself, God almighty, lost in a petty story of its own making. Such is mine “Петре-Еби-Ветре” predicament - it even rhymes when they say it.1

I like letting words work on me like this. Unpack, reverberate, find connections I had overlooked. Sometimes a very familiar phrase has much further to go. Sometimes I get down to embodied basics and, for example, stay with the literal meaning of “let go” to get the most basic sense of it in my body - sometimes staying with such basic stuff helps open up a deeper meaning; don’t underestimate the wisdom of the body.

I was walking with my dog at Kozjak one beautiful sunny day. Two women stopped to pet the dog. I was unusually present to the surroundings, the dog, the people standing with us. “Убав ден,” I said. Beautiful day.

We use it as a familiar greeting in Macedonia, much as we might wish someone a good day. But I meant the day itself. This day, here, with us in it.

They answered casually. I felt a distance between what I had meant and what had been received, and recognized myself in it. Register the phrase, return the phrase, move on. I knew that version of myself very well. For a moment, the flat familiarity and what was available now were close enough to see together.

GPT Astra puts it better than I ever could: “We can remain fluent in the names of things while losing contact with what makes them matter.”

There are degrees of this. I can resonate with a word, understand something of what it points to, feel some of its connections, and still be far from what it might open. Often the label acts as a stopping point  Oh, that’s this. An instance of that. I know it. The recognition gives me confidence that I understand, and somewhere for my attention to stop. As the great Joe Carlsmith puts it:

And if you want, you can stop there; the same way that, if you want, you can glance at a painting, log it as ‘I like it’ or ‘I don’t like it,’ and move on. Or the way you can glance at a forest, or the stars, and say ‘pretty.’ Or the way you can glance at how-to-live and say ‘happiness.’

But you can also do something else. You can look again.

For practical purposes, fine. We need to get things done. But the same movement can leave me feeling that I already grok the world and what life has to offer. The day is unremarkable. The book is boring. The person is saying something I have heard before. I want something more sophisticated, more exciting. Always on the move.

I don’t know that I don’t know. Some of the clarity I would need to notice the limitation is precisely what is missing. It’s a catch-22. In theory, I know better, but still I am usually lost. I meticulously work on maintaining the conditions to stay lost.

This is where faith matters to me. Faith is close to humility: some basic trust that humanity/life/existence is greater than me, that there is something that matters that I cannot currently reach. A willingness to give unearned respect and give something a chance to reach me. This is most fundamental; it is medicine for an illness of cynical arrogance that countless souls suffer from today. That illness of the soul is a non-starter for anything good in life. There has to be room for a basic willingness to open up, trust, respect, care for something beyond you - life, existence, other people, nature, animals, the countless souls before you who also were here - those experiences matter - what traditions are good at is preserving them and finding a way to transmit them across the generations - no matter how lossy and misleading that transmission could get; at the end, nobody/no sect has a monopoly on wisdom2, so staying humble allows something you are currently not in touch with/don’t understand to reach you.

So this is not only about the names of things.  “Can we remain fluent in the representations of things while not losing contact with what they actually represent?”

What stings me about this question when looking back is the basic lack of social connectedness, inconsiderateness/selfishness - staying small and self-preoccupied. Something happened to someone, people talk about it, I hear about it - it’s abstract for me; it barely registers, I tune out, I move on; it does not concern me; it does not touch me; it’s intellectually inefficient to touch me. I was almost autistic by choice. And then, through life circumstances, something happens, an inner or outer change that allows me to get in touch with that, whether I live through it or I just got enough contact with what that representation actually points to, what they were talking about, and what that person actually lived through/experienced. Such is the crudeness of this soul.

This question is live for me today when relating to others. In the past, I basically had something like a mental CRM database registry representation of people - who they are, what they care about, their interests, history, what they did, etc. I was using it to relate to them. But since psychedelics, that has radically changed. I was hit by the rich, vivid, inexhaustible complexity and beauty of what it means to be with another soul. Music transformed from background noise into something as significant as interpersonal encounters. Nature transformed from a place to exercise, mountain-tops to reach, a place to hang out with friends, to a real home - the place where I feel safest and most intimately belong.

How do I practice reaching others these days? I intend to look beyond the priors about that person, especially if they are “bad person” priors, beyond the initial reaction to strangers, beyond our history, beyond the gut feeling - or if you like, I like to let the gut feeling feel for long enough, beyond the power/status game lenses, beyond what I expect this encounter to give me, beyond what is expected of me, beyond what I think this encounter is about. Enough room, enough trust in the moment-by-moment unfolding of the encounter itself, receive, give room to the person in front of me to let whatever happens happen. This is a practice; ideally, there is no intention; you are just there.

I was a hard nut to crack. I spent two months of meditation retreat practice carrying “just sitting” as a reminder, an instruction, a key that would unlock some door. Prior to that I spent, months preparing for it by reading books. Later durring that retreat I mentioned the nun that I was preparing for a warzone by reading combat instruction manuals. Only later did I hear “just sitting” as the most ordinary expression of the no-thing itself. There was no door to enter. Just sitting, nothing special. (The early Chan fathers are weeping: “dude thinks he is cracked, may God help him.”)

Part of what draws me to practice is the loosening of that pressure to achieve something, to figure out something, to get better, (and if you read carefully) to be special. Less pressure to manage myself, less pressure to manage anything. The seeking-answers obsession, the gripping of the need to understand, the dream of salvation, the circular concern with myself, seeing everything through that lens. How much precious juice/aliveness/soul-compute goes toward that God of I, I, I, me, me, me, my, my, my?

Stop for a second. Imagine what it would be like if you were to, just for a few moments, get back to childhood. No problems to solve, nothing to figure out, no practice, no path, no worth to prove, no image to maintain. Remember that? There was no quest, no agenda; we were just there, playing, wondering, being alive. 3

Just being. If anything is freedom, that’s freedom. What stops you from letting go of all this shit? Remember. One day you will have to let go of everything dear to you anyway; how much of it can you let go of now? It’s your life, FGS!

Other representations have opened by asking something of me. I returned to Christian prayer after Dharma practice. I am not religious in the conventional sense. I do it anyway, with whatever sincerity I can gather. Lately, after a period of exploring Tibetan deity practice, I have found it easier to enter that relationship more imaginatively and sincerely. If you like, I switched White Tara with Jesus Christ.

 The pragmatic dharma dude yelling in my head, “do this to get that”, “now this is that stage”, “now let’s make this move,” has loosened a little since. That voice has taken up quite some space in my soul. It has its uses too. But you won’t believe in how, rare, significant, sacred moments of my life, that dude was able to come up to model what is going on. These days I have a healthier relationship with all the voices in my mind; I just let them do their thing without giving them much importance.

There can also be playful examination of the voices. During that same retreat, while sitting for long periods of time accompanied by unbearable physical pain, the parliament of voices rebelled; the pragmatic dharma dude in my head suggested amplifying them to the max - there were screams; pain intensified. But somehow this hapenstance was really amusing, - the pragmatic dharma dude screamed together with them - in his characteristic voice, his head on fire. Hilarious. After a while, you see how insubstantial they are. Pain is still there, but aversion to it progressively subsides until it is perceived as energy. It becomes fuel; then you ask for it, you look for it to anchor your efforts. It actually is a natural anchor for me (at least this kind of meditation-related pain): it both gives attention something stable and energizes your efforts.

Anyway,  back to prayer. Having tapped into the treasure of one wisdom tradition, I wanted to see what another had to offer now that I am in touch with this playful, imaginal way of looking at and relating to phenomena/energies/archetypes in my experience. Switching Tara with Jesus Christ somehow magically made a huge difference for me in the exploratory Deity Practice efforts I made. For quite some time I wanted to get back to the one tradition that had already influenced me without me choosing it. There was also a kind of longing for your kind; Those icons are still in my heart, somehow that ortodox icon style hits different than any other visual representation.

No matter how badly misguided this looked to the ratio: “all sentient beings are one kind, all humans are one kind”, and yet I felt differently for my native Ortodox church fathers lineage ancestors - so I leaned into this partiality. There must be a reason, I thought, that so much attention goes into asking for mercy and forgiveness.

“God, forgive me.”

Earlier I connected forgiveness mostly with repairing social ruptures, apologizing and regretting something. I associated repentance with regret at final judgment. Then I actually spent time doing this. Alone, present, letting the body, mind, and heart settle, returning to the words, the intention behind the words with as much sincerity as I could gather.

Later I felt that this is basically a purification practice. The force I relate to, whatever it is, my deep inner wisdom beyond my conscious reach, begins to touch things I am ordinarily so good at avoiding. What I don’t want to see, what I can look away from so swiftly that I hardly notice the movement. Asking brings some of it forth, makes it bare, makes it sting. Sometimes, in being seen this way, it loosens its hold. What surprises me is how this kind of practice has somehow more influence than Dharma practice over my self-defeating/self-limiting/self-depricating/self-degrading patterns. It straightens me; I just naturally get my shit together, take daily life more seriously - everything starts to count - every thought, every intention, every little deed, I find myself surprised doing the right things habitually without trying to. Why is this working so much better on me, God knows. I am kind of a Christian, I guess.

“God… please… have mercy on me.”

There is something genius in the relational part of this. Even alone, I am asking. It touches an emotional, social, even hierarchical/child-parent/metaphysical capacity in me: appealing, needing help, putting myself in relation to someone, something, tapping into powers that are apparently beyond my own personal reach. Devotion reaches places that my more investigative, contemplative traditions and philosophical inquiry practices have never taken me. Describing the prayer as an attention exercise, or inner programming, leaves out so much of what happens in the asking and after it gets going.

The words give the thinking mind something to do. When it wanders, return to the prayer. I am already used to thinking, intending, feeling; here those capacities have somewhere to go besides the usual proliferation. As attention steadies, I can begin to recognize the words with my body and heart as well as my mind. This is part of what I mean by cultivation doing the legwork.

Then when things get fluid, I can play with the form. An open-ended, improvising prayer. A blessing for someone. What feels right to say now? What intention does this moment call for? I listen deeply while I say it, out loud or in myself. An intention begins to find words; hearing them changes what I can intend. Sometimes I say it differently. Sometimes I catch myself not meaning it. Sometimes I catch myself saying something and meaning something else. Sometimes I catch others influencing me with a meaning different from what they are saying. Sometimes I stay with the same words and mean them more fully.

I called this programming when I was trying to explain it. Our habitual thinking is already shaping us, often blindly; here we take the wheel a little.  And “thinking” doesn’t quite capture it either. It can be an expression of the heart, an expression of wisdom. Self-expression in a loop.

It’s creative, wholesome and lovely until you notice someone speaking through you, or, more precisely, responding in the exact right way without intending it. Without interfering with the process of it. Even getting surprised by it most often in the good sense, but sometimes in the bad sense - I feel like sometimes it lacks kindness. It can feel like an apparently “higher, deeper, wiser” will, with little interest in my agenda or the agendas of those around me, or cultural expectations, the field responding to that moment. What I mean is that sometimes my own words and actions arrive as something I hear/witness. This is not to say that there is no value in calculated, deliberate, slow, intentional, thoughtful relating to stuff. There is much strength in that too. But this bouncy, spontaneous, momentary otherness  is something less known to me.

Do some real efforts at prayer and ordinary speech begins to sound different too. People may never pray formally, but you cannot escape prayer; we go on intending, thinking, speaking all day long. What are we praying for? What do the words serve? Goodness, love, fear, greed, pride? Sometimes I hear someone finding words they mean. Sometimes I hear the habitual words, almost going by themselves. I hear it in myself too. Which god is getting all this practice?

A word or a way of looking can deepen contact. It can also limit what I allow myself to encounter. I want room for both movements: entering a form that helps something become possible, and letting my hold on it loosen when the situation asks for something else.

During a retreat with my wife, we tried an exercise she had been reading about: sit on chairs facing each other, look into each other’s eyes, give it time, get a little closer. Then a little closer. We were about a week into the retreat, practising for several hours a day.

There was quiet music. After ten minutes or so, we were holding hands, knees together. I was making small, soft circles with my body from the seat bones. She was moving too. We mirrored each other. Then we stood up and continued looking.

I felt as though I was meeting her for the first time. The vibrancy of her soul in her eyes. I was surprised by how attractive she was. We had shared deep intimacy before, but I hadn’t appreciated her like this.

We stayed there for a few minutes. The cultivation had done the groundwork. There was very little effort now, no pushing or pulling, just remaining open to her.

On the same retreat we tried an instruction about connecting our chakras while making love. The closeness we were reaching for became possible when we stopped trying to follow it. The instruction had helped loosen our habits and given us some faith that there was a there there. Then there was what the actual moment required, which a rigid, lossy instruction couldn’t quite tell us. Trusting the body, staying in tune with what was happening between us.

We had had intense encounters earlier with psychedelics too, some even deeper. But we had been out of our depth, given resonance and intimacy without really knowing how to process or live with what had happened. Trying to distinguish this, I reached for “earning it.” I meant getting the conditions right, developing the capacity to integrate an experience. Earning it through effort and pain wasn’t the point.

We haven’t returned to that particular intimacy in making love. And that’s actually okay. Grasping after it isn’t the point either. It leaves stronger faith in what’s possible. I can remember looking at my wife in considerable detail and still be unable to meet her in the same way now.

The memory of access can outlast the access.

In chapter 5 of *Seeing That Frees*, Rob Burbea describes an insight losing its liberating power until we are left with “only a shell, a memory of an experience.” He writes about returning to insights, acting from them, and cultivating the collectedness and well-being of samādhi so they can take root. Sometimes, he suggests, later cultivation can bring an earlier insight back to life. I recognize the difficulty he is addressing: remembering that something mattered, without having available what once made it so clear.

Over years of practice, I have developed more trust in going through the dry stretches. A day when the words feel empty doesn’t carry quite the authority it once did. There may be more than I can reach today. This is faith too: returning without demanding that a previous encounter repeat itself.

But the person in that memory is also the person I live with. I can remember the beauty of my wife’s soul and, absorbed in a project, feel her presence as a distraction, a limiting condition. It’s stupid, but it’s honestly how I sometimes feel. She is here, available, and I take that convenience for granted.

How do I live the intimacy? I wanted intimacy to emerge organically, through sheer presence and our ordinary ways of living. I have even deliberately avoided being the regular present-buying, attention-giving husband. But why not nudge it a little? Take her on a date? More fundamentally, do less of the distracting stuff. Less doing obsessions, less productivity, less following the AI frontier. I am the dude who goes all in, from morning until late in the day. It is partly a strength and partly a weakness. Knowing what matters hasn’t made me very good at leaving room for it.

Thinking about these encounters has brought me to something beyond what the retreat itself was about. How do I live the basic human intimacy with everyone? I want to let others affect me. Their words, presence, actions, energy. Give their worlds room in mine, even when it feels as if they are interfering with my project or my agenda. I have been the individualist with a wall, taking it as a strength that I cannot be moved. Lately I have felt the limitation in that. I want to give others space to, in some sense, claim me. Become available to the situation. Responsive.

This intimacy/availability goes deeper and wider than any particular relation. The psychedelic phase, the mystical openings that come with it, and contemplative practice inform me about the possibility and the imperative to open up, be intimate with all experiences.

Beauty, pleasure, joy, humour, profound states, bliss, and pain, fear, grief, loss, anger, conflict, disgust. Neutral, uneventful, ordinary states. Opening to and illuminating all phenomena as they actually are, becoming available to the full range of life, without immediately dodging, recoiling, pushing, pulling, numbing, demanding that it be something else. Inhabiting the human condition as it is, beyond the label “the human condition,” on its own terms.

Let the encounter with suffering reach me; let me stay there without trying to solve it, fix it, or run away from it, let me acknowledge the truth of suffering. Let death reach me, let the fact that sooner or later I will have to part with everyone and everything dear to me reach me; let me be able to just remain there in that knowing; let me live that; let me learn how to die before I die, let me give up the war, fundamentally, there is no enemy, there is no separation. Let fear, suffering, brokenness, confusion, insecurity, shame, shadow reach.

This possibility/imperative does not undermine practical matters,  your common sense, and the need to take care of yourself, your survival, health, well-being, and the care of your loved ones. Read that last sentence again.


Let truth claim me too, wherever it comes from. An argument, a perspective, a take. What is the ground for it? Does it make sense? I want to give it room, including when it comes from AI. I am willing to risk my voice, what I thought I actually had to say, to go deeper, be challenged, let a relevant perspective work on me, comperhensively in a systematic way, go through all considerations given the matter - without losing ground to analysis-paralysis.

We grew these machines, in part, on our own written words. There is some trust in that too. And trust in the contemporary priests working on them. Thinking and writing with AI is where I am trying to do that most explicitly. Let the encounter change what I can say, and be intellectually honest about what emerges.

And now, we have genies that could mirror/relate to/empower those parts and help them grow over time and even take over. May God have mercy on us. Are we going to take our collective shit together as things start getting alarming? Or are we collectively going toward the brink of collective wire-heading done wrong?

Agency, as I use the word, is the ingenuity of achieving a goal, getting something done, given that you set out to get it done. Now that our agency is empowered by these increasingly capable agents/goal achieving entities, the question: “what deserves commitment? “- becomes even more relevant. These radical openness teachings can sound very wise and wholesome. There are difficulties when you care enough to try to live them before you are mature enough to do so. As Daniel Ingram (that pragmatic dharma dude that yells in my head) says: practice is like trying to reprogram the operating system while it’s running - if you ask your tech-bro friends, they will tell you that’s not the safest practice, but that’s the best we could get out of our own situation. One Buddhist teacher (I forgot who) has said we practice to make the best of a bad situation.

For a week I deeply intended to be in the room with everyone, to let their worlds enter mine, to remain receptive, open, vulnerable. There it is, the distance between knowing and being able to live it. The lesson I could see afterward was to give without demanding it in return. I don’t actually know how to write a single coherent essay; I was never taught how to do it formally. Here is the ending I intended; right now, after all this freewriting, I think it doesn’t quite match. Here it is anyway, since I find it really close to my heart.

One day I was on the couch with my niece, who is about two. Her parents and my wife were at the table. I put on the “Bare Necessities” scene from *The Jungle Book*. I wanted to be the algorithm for her, choosing something I thought worth her attention.

We watched the bear and the boy. At some point, “receive” crossed my mind, a word I had used in practice. Some capacity to stay, to leave breathing room, was available with it.

The closeness developed gradually. Then she leaned against me.

I hadn’t expected that gesture of trust from her. The others noticed, commented, and recorded us. We were enjoying the cartoon. I was amused.

In telling this at first, I got carried away. Numinous, magical, all kinds of maximalist epithets. Then I had to stop. There was no great mystical realization on that couch. A regular day, regular consciousness, enjoying the cartoon. When I remember it, I am moved.

Only afterward did I dwell on the resemblance: the bear and the child on the screen, the two of us on the couch. Perhaps sharing the scene helped us be close. Intimacy can need an anchor, something to attend to together while it develops in its own way. I can’t say how much the cartoon did. The possibility interests me. An image can help us enter a relationship, and so can choosing what to put on and staying beside someone while they watch.

I have invested much less of myself in these ordinary parts of life. Here was ordinary belonging. This actually is the practice. There is a huge difference between knowing what I am writing here and being able to access it. No, not even access it on demand. Just living it. I want to leave some crumbs on the path that someone could follow. Me too.

Remembering can work this way too. Sometimes an experience recedes beyond the reach of its memory. Sometimes I return to a memory and begin to appreciate something I barely noticed while it happened. I am writing partly to give these things more of a chance to reach me.

That evening, she leaned into me and we went on watching.
