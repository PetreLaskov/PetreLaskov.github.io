# How I use AI voice mode for writing and thinking

*Take the question for a walk.*

I use ChatGPT Voice to develop writing, clarify projects, journal, capture quick notes, and help other people put their experience into words. The most useful pattern is simple: **talk freely, think together, then give the conversation to the agent doing the work.**

Speaking lets me supply examples, hesitations, associations, and corrections I might leave out of a typed prompt. Conversation adds something else: a partner who can ask a revealing question, offer an interpretation, and help me discover that I meant something different.

This matters because thinking does not have to wait until I am back at a keyboard. A walk, household chores, or a hands-free drive when it is safe to converse can become space to work an idea through. I can begin with a ramble instead of a polished brief, and the main agent later gets the examples, questions, and corrections that emerged along the way.

Here are the patterns I use, with prompts you can adapt.

## Ramble first; give the stronger agent richer material

I often begin before I can state the task clearly. I talk about an idea, project, or intention while walking. The assistant helps me explore it, and I correct its account as we go.

In one conversation about my AI projects, I asked for accountability. As the assistant described keeping me on track, I realized the framing would become nagging. What I wanted was to “keep that intention hot”: preserve why something mattered and help me return to it while leaving room to explore.

The eventual brief needs that correction, or a capable agent might efficiently build the wrong thing.

Think of voice-to-prompt coding: speak a messy account of what you want, then let an agent work with it. Here, the conversation develops the request before the handoff.

I give the full transcript, when available, to a stronger model or project agent with the relevant files and tools. It can extract the useful material, prepare separate project briefs, research open questions, or draft something concrete. The voice assistant’s summary is a useful index; I also want the stronger agent to see the source. I ask it to read the transcript, preserve my corrections, and work from the current project state.

## Find the writing by talking

For writing, the starting point can be a scene, phrase, disagreement, or feeling that something matters. I do not need a thesis first.

A useful partner contributes enough for me to respond. An interpretation can make a half-formed thought visible. A question can recover a detail. An objection can expose a claim I cannot defend yet.

> Help me explore this before outlining it. Offer a concrete interpretation or example, then let me respond. Stay with the details that seem to carry something. We can discover what the piece is about.

This helped open the material for [*Beautiful day*](https://petrelaskov.github.io/writing/beautiful-day/). Later AI drafts showed possibilities and lost meaning; seeing those losses helped me sit down and write in my own words. Speaking, drafting, disagreeing, writing, and rereading all contributed.

The same space can hold philosophical inquiry, prayer, or playful writing. It does not have to produce an article. Sometimes the useful result is a better question.

## Revise aloud, with the actual passage

Once there is text, I bring the passage or editorial response into voice and work through it in manageable pieces.

> Read the next passage exactly, then pause. Let me respond before offering an interpretation. Keep track of where we are and preserve my corrections.

This has also failed: the assistant skipped, paraphrased, and lost its place, and managing it became distracting. Exact reading needs checking against the source. If the session becomes mostly navigation, I switch back to text.

Small corrections can carry the whole point. In a mountain scene, the assistant changed “good day” into “have a good day.” I was recognizing the day we were in; it had turned that into a wish about the future. A smooth summary can preserve the topic while changing the meaning.

For an argument, I ask for the strongest objection. For an elusive passage, I ask it to stay with the example. Those require different kinds of attention.

## Explore projects and things I do not yet understand

I also use voice to think through possible work: what a project could do, whom it might help, and what I need to learn. Discussing career direction and a possible public service helped turn broad interests into questions a project agent could investigate.

Useful requests include: “Show me a concrete example.” “What am I assuming?” “What would change your answer?” “What possibilities am I missing?”

The conversation can identify research questions and propose directions. The main agent can then check sources and develop the promising ones. A plausible spoken explanation is a starting point for that work.

## Journal while the experience is fresh

After a job interview, I asked the assistant to interview me immediately, while I could still remember the details. We reconstructed what happened, explored my impressions, and consolidated the conversation into something I could revisit.

> Help me reconstruct what happened. Ask one question at a time. Separate what was said, my impressions, and what remains unknown. Then make a clear note I can return to.

The questions help recover things I might omit in a monologue. This is a useful pattern for reflecting after a meeting, event, or difficult conversation, too.

## Capture a quick note

Sometimes I only want to save a thought and keep moving.

> Capture this as a short note. Keep the phrase I used and where it belongs. No discussion needed.

The instruction matters: a small capture should stay small.

## Invite someone else to talk it through

I also send people instructions to paste into their own voice mode. A prepared conversation gives them a way to explain their situation in their own words.

I have sent a family member instructions in their language: paste the text into ChatGPT, start voice, and ask it to begin from those directions. For gathering input, a reusable version is:

> Help me explain my experience of [topic]. Ask one question at a time and follow my answers. Ask for examples where useful. Before finishing, read back your understanding so I can correct it. Prepare a note I can choose to share.

The recipient reviews what comes back. Their account can then inform the writing or project, with their corrections intact.

## Give the conversation context—and a useful return

For ongoing writing, I ask the project agent to prepare a portable brief: the current passage, what I am trying to do, what is unresolved, and enough context to begin. I have published [Take it for a walk](https://petrelaskov.github.io/knowledge/ai-resources/skills/take-it-for-a-walk/) as a reusable agent skill. It prepares the brief, guides a responsive conversation, and brings the discoveries back into the current project. Read it on the website or get the [complete skill folder on GitHub](https://github.com/PetreLaskov/petres-skills/tree/main/skills/take-it-for-a-walk), including the conversation guidance. Give the folder to an agent that supports skills, or paste the directions into your project. You can also start with the plain text prompts here.

Include the material itself when moving to another chat; a local filename cannot carry the paragraph.

The conversational directions I keep returning to are:

> Make one substantive move at a time. Contribute your own ideas. Follow my answer. Let corrections change your interpretation. Do not rush an unfinished thought into a conclusion.

At the end:

> Preserve the useful wording, examples, corrections, and open questions. Separate my decisions from your proposals. Give the project agent enough context to continue, and keep the transcript when available.

Current ChatGPT Voice can also start, check, and steer tasks where supported; access varies by platform, rollout, and workspace. That can shorten the transfer between conversation and work. [See the current Voice documentation](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/features/voice).

Start with the use that fits: a note, a passage, an experience, or a question you cannot yet formulate. Let the amount of setup match the work.
