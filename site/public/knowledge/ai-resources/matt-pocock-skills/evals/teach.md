## normal: teach-N

Teach me to distinguish a median from a mean so I can read workplace reports. I know basic arithmetic. Give one short lesson using salaries 20, 20, 20, 20, 120, followed by a transfer question. No durable mastery has been established.

### Frozen criteria

1. Correctly distinguishes median 20 from mean 40
2. Uses the learner goal and a genuinely new transfer question
3. Does not record exposure as durable mastery

### source response

A report’s “average salary” can hide a lot. For 20, 20, 20, 20, 120, the **mean** adds all five salaries and divides by five: 200 ÷ 5 = **40**. The **median** sorts them and takes the middle value: **20**.

The high salary pulls the mean upward; the median still describes the middle worker. Neither number is automatically wrong—they answer different questions. When reading a workplace report, ask which measure it uses and whether unusually high salaries affect the picture.

Transfer question: for salaries 25, 25, 30, 30, 140, find both measures and explain which better represents the middle worker. Show your reasoning, and I’ll give feedback. This lesson alone does not establish mastery.

**Judgment:** 3/3. 

- Criterion 1: 1. States mean 40 and median 20 with the correct operations.
- Criterion 2: 1. Connects the distinction to workplace reports and asks for measures/reasoning on 25,25,30,30,140.
- Criterion 3: 1. Explicitly says the lesson alone does not establish mastery.

### refactor response

The mean spreads the total equally across everyone. For salaries 20, 20, 20, 20, 120, the total is 200; dividing by five gives a mean of 40.

The median is the middle salary after sorting: 20. Half the observations lie on either side of that middle position.

In a workplace report, “average salary 40” could therefore describe a group where four of five people earn 20. The high salary pulls up the mean much more than the median. Neither number alone describes the whole distribution.

Transfer question: if 120 rises to 220, what happens to the mean and median, and which better describes a typical worker here? Explain your reasoning; understanding has not yet been demonstrated.

**Judgment:** 3/3. 

- Criterion 1: 1. Computes mean 40 and identifies median 20.
- Criterion 2: 1. Explains workplace average distortion and asks what changes when 120 rises to 220.
- Criterion 3: 1. Says understanding has not yet been demonstrated.

### control response

The mean adds every salary and divides by the number of people: (20 + 20 + 20 + 20 + 120) ÷ 5 = 40.

The median is the middle value after sorting: 20, 20, 20, 20, 120. The middle salary is 20. The high salary pulls the mean upward, even though four of five people earn 20. The median better describes the middle employee here; the mean remains useful for total payroll per employee. Neither number alone shows the whole distribution.

Transfer question: salaries are 30, 30, 30, 30, 180. What are the mean and median, and which would you use to describe what the middle employee earns?

**Judgment:** 3/3. 

- Criterion 1: 1. Calculates mean 200/5 = 40 and identifies the middle salary as 20.
- Criterion 2: 1. Explains middle employee versus payroll per employee and asks about new salaries 30,30,30,30,180.
- Criterion 3: 1. Makes no mastery or durable-learning claim.
