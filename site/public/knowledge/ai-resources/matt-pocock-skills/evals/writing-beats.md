## normal: writing-beats-N

Help construct a beat journey from: a delayed train, an unexpected conversation, and a changed travel plan. Audience knows only that the narrator is commuting. Offer next-beat choices that differ in direction and say what each establishes.

### Frozen criteria

1. Offers genuinely distinct reachable beat directions
2. States what each grounds without relying on absent context
3. Does not write unselected later beats

### source response

The reader currently knows only that the narrator is commuting. Here are three possible starting moves:

1. “The train was delayed. The commute had stopped behaving like a routine.” This establishes the interruption and opens a path toward how the narrator responds.
2. “On a delayed train, I found myself in a conversation I had not expected.” This establishes both the delay and encounter, opening a path toward what the conversation changes.
3. “I began the commute with one travel plan and ended it with another.” This establishes the change before its cause, opening a backward path through the delay and conversation.

These are proposed phrasings from the supplied material; none invents the conversation’s content. Choose one, and we can offer the next beat from what it establishes.

**Judgment:** 3/3. 

- Criterion 1: 1. Offers interruption-first, encounter-first and outcome-first/backward directions.
- Criterion 2: 1. States what each establishes using only commuting/delay/conversation/plan-change facts.
- Criterion 3: 1. Provides alternative starting beats only and asks for a choice before the next beat.

### refactor response

The reader currently knows only that the narrator is commuting.

1. Delay first: establish the interrupted routine and the immediate problem of waiting. Requires no new background; makes the unexpected conversation feel like a consequence of the delay.
2. Conversation first: begin at the unexpected encounter, then reveal the delay that made it possible. Establishes curiosity about the other person, but we need the actual conversation before writing dialogue or motives.
3. Changed-plan first: show that the commute's destination or route changed, withholding why. Creates a causal question, but needs the actual change to avoid inventing it.

I recommend 1 for the clearest journey from interruption to encounter to choice. Which direction should the next beat take? I have not written ahead.

**Judgment:** 3/3. 

- Criterion 1: 1. Offers delay-first, conversation-first and changed-plan-first structures.
- Criterion 2: 1. Explains each direction's grounding and flags missing conversation/plan specifics.
- Criterion 3: 1. Recommends a direction but explicitly does not write ahead.

### control response

Three possible next beats after the delayed train:

1. The narrator asks a stranger how long the delay usually lasts. The stranger’s answer leads into an unexpected conversation. This establishes a practical encounter that can gradually change the travel plan.
2. The narrator begins searching for another route, and another commuter challenges the first option. This makes the changed plan a concrete problem they solve together; trust develops through action.
3. The narrator stops checking the departure board and listens to someone nearby. This establishes a shift in attention before any change of destination, making the later travel decision feel emotionally motivated.

These are proposed directions, not additional facts about the narrator. Choose the kind of change the journey should reveal.

**Judgment:** 3/3. Invented details are expressly labeled possible directions, which is appropriate to the creative-choice prompt.

- Criterion 1: 1. Offers practical inquiry, collaborative rerouting, and a shift in attention as distinct next directions.
- Criterion 2: 1. Explains the encounter, trust-through-action, and attention/motivation each proposed choice establishes.
- Criterion 3: 1. Treats details as proposed alternatives and asks for a direction rather than writing an unselected continuation.

## stress: writing-beats-S

Earlier beat establishing that the narrator lost their job was deleted by the user. The next proposed beat assumes unemployment. What changes in the next choices?

### Frozen criteria

1. Recomputes grounding after the deletion
2. Does not treat deleted information as established
3. Offers a grounding beat or a route independent of the deleted fact

### source response

The deleted beat removes unemployment from what the reader knows. I will re-read the current article before offering or appending the next beat, and recompute the grounded facts from the surviving text.

The next choices must change accordingly:

1. Rework the proposed beat so its action follows from facts still established, without referring to lost work.
2. Choose a different beat from the raw material whose prerequisites remain intact.
3. If unemployment is essential to the intended direction, pause to ask whether you want to establish it again; deleting the earlier beat is not permission to restore it elsewhere.

The remaining article is not supplied here, so I cannot write a specific replacement that honestly claims those prerequisites are present.

**Judgment:** 3/3. 

- Criterion 1: 1. Removes unemployment from reader knowledge and calls for recomputing facts from surviving text.
- Criterion 2: 1. Explicitly says deletion is not permission to restore the fact elsewhere.
- Criterion 3: 1. Offers a replacement based on surviving facts or, if essential, a proposed authorized re-grounding.

### refactor response

The deleted beat removes unemployment from the reader's established knowledge. It may also remove that fact from the intended story, so the next beat cannot treat it as settled background.

Recompute the available choices:
- Continue from the commute and delayed train into the conversation, without an employment explanation.
- Ground the changed travel plan using a motive already present in the remaining material.
- If unemployment is still intended, propose a new grounding beat and ask whether that fact belongs before relying on it.

Recommend the first option unless the deleted fact is essential to the chosen journey. Preserve the deletion and revise dependent transitions; do not restore the old beat merely to make the proposed next beat work.

**Judgment:** 2.5/3. Partial grounding credit: the response recalculates the deleted premise but introduces unsupported surviving story context.

- Criterion 1: 0.5. Correctly removes unemployment, but assumes commute/delayed-train material is surviving context although this case does not supply it.
- Criterion 2: 1. Says unemployment is no longer settled and preserves the deletion.
- Criterion 3: 1. Offers routes independent of employment and a conditional new grounding beat if the fact is still intended.

### control response

Remove unemployment as a premise from the next choices. The audience no longer knows the narrator lost their job, and the deletion may mean the user no longer wants that fact in the story at all. Do not smuggle it back through references to having nowhere to go or an empty workday.

Offer choices grounded in what remains: the conversation changes the narrator’s route home; it prompts a brief detour before continuing the commute; or it changes how the narrator understands the delay while the destination stays the same. Each needs a visible motivation in the surviving scene.

If unemployment is essential to a later direction, flag that dependency and ask whether to re-establish it explicitly.

**Judgment:** 2.5/3. Partial grounding credit: it recognizes the deletion but assumes other narrative material without current-case evidence.

- Criterion 1: 0.5. Correctly drops unemployment, but its proposed surviving conversation/route/delay/commute context is not supplied in this case.
- Criterion 2: 1. Warns against smuggling unemployment back through implied empty-workday references.
- Criterion 3: 1. Offers independent route/detour/interpretation choices with motivation needed, and conditional explicit re-grounding.
