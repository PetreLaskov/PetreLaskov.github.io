# Stop rule

Identify the user's required outcome and the decision the work serves. Preserve explicit requirements; a stopping rule cannot redefine them downward.

Distinguish blockers from desirable improvements. State the minimum observable condition that makes the result usable, the uncertainty acceptable at that point, and what finding would require more work.

Choose a review point proportionate to cost. Use natural milestones rather than arbitrary counters when a fixed count would truncate necessary work. Honor any user budget or deadline.

At the review point, compare current evidence with the rule. Continue for a specific unresolved blocker, deliver when the condition is met, or report why the task cannot presently meet it. Do not treat fatigue, output volume, or polished presentation as completion.

If the rule itself was wrong, revise it openly with the reason. Do not endlessly move the finish line to accommodate optional improvements.
