# Design the Learning Sequence

Sequence learning by demonstrated capability, not merely topic order.

1. Identify the target performance: what the learner should be able to explain, decide, or do in a new case.
2. Establish current understanding from available work or a small representative exercise. Do not infer enduring traits from one answer.
3. Choose the next exercise that exposes the most important missing distinction. Useful forms include explain, apply, compare, debug, construct a counterexample, and adapt.
4. Give the learner a concrete task, necessary source material, and a clear criterion for success without giving away the answer.
5. Evaluate the output against that criterion. Separate factual gaps, conceptual confusion, execution mistakes, and unsupported confidence.
6. Adapt the next task. Use a contrasting case when fluent explanation does not transfer; avoid assigning more reading by default.
7. End with a fresh transfer case and a concise account of demonstrated versus still-unproven capability.

Keep the initial sequence short and revisable. No elaborate learner profile is needed. Success is improved performance on a new case, not finishing a syllabus or repeating vocabulary.
