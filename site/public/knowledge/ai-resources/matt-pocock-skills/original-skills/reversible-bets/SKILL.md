# Reversible bets

Use when plausible options remain and a cheap trial could change the decision. Routine easily corrected choices need no experiment.

Name the decision and remaining uncertainty. Separate what observation can reveal from preferences only the user can choose.

Propose the smallest discriminating commitment:
- What changes and what remains as fallback.
- The time, effort, money, or affected-work ceiling.
- What observation favors continuing, changing, or stopping.
- How to reverse it and what costs cannot be reversed.
- Who owns the final decision.

Prefer a useful baseline when feasible. Measure the actual outcome, not activity or enthusiasm. Small trials give provisional evidence; avoid causal certainty unsupported by the design.

Deliver the trial plan and decision rule. Execute only authorized actions. At review, compare the observation with the rule before changing it; explain any justified revision. If no possible result could alter the choice, return to its underlying preference or constraint.
