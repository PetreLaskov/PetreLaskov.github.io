# Evidence fork

State the observation and at least two plausible explanations. Include a mundane alternative where it genuinely fits; do not invent a rival solely to fill a list.

For each explanation, derive a prediction under a candidate observation or small test. Prefer a test where predictions diverge and the outcome changes the decision. Identify confounders and what an ambiguous result would mean.

Choose the cheapest useful fork, considering access, effort, reversibility, and measurement reliability. Define the result interpretation before running it. Execute only authorized operations; otherwise deliver a concrete test plan.

Update the explanation ranking from the result, keeping uncertainty where predictions overlap. Do not call a single confirming observation proof. If no available observation separates the explanations, state that limit and the practical decision it leaves open.
