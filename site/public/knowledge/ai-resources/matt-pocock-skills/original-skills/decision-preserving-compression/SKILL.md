# Decision-preserving compression

Use when length must fall but the reader still needs to act. Ordinary stylistic shortening does not need this skill.

1. Establish the destination reader and size constraint. Identify decisions they must make.
2. Extract commitments affecting those decisions: actor, action, condition, exception, status, and decisive reason. Preserve refusals and uncertainty. Keep a source locator for disputed meanings.
3. Compress narrative, repetitions, and examples first. Replace settled detail with usable references. Preserve exact values, ordering guarantees, and exclusions that change behavior.
4. Read as the destination reader. Try a normal case and a boundary case. Check whether the result permits what the original forbade, forbids what it permitted, or turns an assumption into a decision.
5. Return the compact artifact and material omissions or ambiguities. If the requested size cannot carry the contract, say so and provide the smallest version that can.

A dangling pointer is deletion, not compression. Do not invent a commitment to make the result decisive.

Example: preserve “two retries before acknowledgment; manual approval afterward” rather than reducing it to “retry failed deliveries.”
