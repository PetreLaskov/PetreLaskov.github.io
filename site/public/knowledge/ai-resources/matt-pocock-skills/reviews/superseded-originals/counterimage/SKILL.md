name: counterimage
description: "Strengthen an essay or argument by developing the concrete case that most seriously complicates its central claim."
---

# Counterimage

Use when a draft's central claim feels compelling but too easy. Produce one concrete challenge that can improve the claim, not a compulsory balanced debate.

Read the thesis and its strongest supplied example. State what the thesis predicts and which condition would make that prediction fail. Look first in the user's material for an actual contrary scene. If none exists, propose a clearly hypothetical case or identify a factual case that needs verification. Do not invent lived experience or quotations.

Choose the challenge that pressures the claim's central mechanism. An extreme exception with no relation to the intended audience is less useful than an ordinary situation the argument overlooks. Describe the scene briefly enough that the author can see what happens, why it matters, and which sentence in the draft it troubles.

Offer three possible repairs only when they are genuinely different: narrow the claim's scope, divide two mechanisms that the draft conflates, or retain the claim and explain why the challenge does not defeat it. Recommend the repair that preserves the most explanatory power with the fewest evasions.

Write a proposed replacement thesis and one paragraph showing how the counterimage enters the piece. Keep facts and hypotheticals explicit. If the user asked for edits, revise the relevant passage and its affected transitions; otherwise present the proposal. Do not turn every objection into a caveat in the final prose.

Finish by naming one case the revised thesis now explains better and one observation that would still overturn it. Stop when the thesis has a meaningful boundary; searching indefinitely for objections is not the task.
