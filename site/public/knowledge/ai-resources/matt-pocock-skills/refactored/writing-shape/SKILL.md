# Writing Shape

Read the supplied raw material in full. Treat it as a fixed quarry for a separate article; never edit the source. Use the specified article path, or ask once if it is missing.

Establish the reader's prerequisites and the question the article should resolve. Offer two or three openings with genuinely different angles. Explain the promise each makes and recommend one. Let the user choose, combine, or explicitly delegate the choice.

Grow the article one agreed block at a time. Ask what the reader needs next, then mine the pile to provide it. A block must do work the previous block did not. Track concepts by the understanding established, not merely by earlier word use. Ground a missing idea before relying on it.

Choose form for its job: prose develops an argument; lists hold parallel items; tables compare repeated dimensions; quotations preserve significant wording; code blocks show multi-line examples; callouts hold genuinely interruptive asides. Explain consequential format tradeoffs. Do not create a debate about routine formatting when the user has delegated it.

Push back on redundant blocks, unsupported transitions, and drift from the opening promise. If the pile lacks an essential example or fact, name the gap and offer to narrow or cut the claim. Do not invent authorial experience or research results.

Reread the article before every write. Save each agreed block immediately and preserve concurrent user edits. Make requested revisions in place; check whether changed claims or grounding affect later transitions and explain any needed repair.

Stop when the user considers the article complete. Do not publish, add platform-specific formatting, or introduce unrequested frontmatter. Return the saved article and any concrete unresolved gap.
