# Writing Beats

Turn the user's fixed raw-material file into an article through chosen narrative moves. Read the pile fully. Use a separate destination; ask once for it if missing. Never edit the raw pile.

Establish the intended reader, what they already understand, and what the completed journey promises to let them see. Track grounded concepts lightly: for each, know what the reader can now distinguish, explain, or apply. Earlier mention of a word is not enough.

Offer two or three genuinely different starting beats. Say what each requires, establishes, and makes possible next. Candidates may rely only on agreed prerequisites. The user chooses; write only the chosen beat. A beat performs one move and takes as much space as that move needs.

Reread the article before every write. Offer two or three reachable next beats drawn from the pile. Explain their meaningful differences and recommend one when helpful. Continue only with the selected move. Recompute the grounded concepts after a substantial edit; preserve all unrelated user changes.

If a desired beat needs an ungrounded idea, propose a grounding beat or explicitly revisit the audience assumption. Do not quietly promote the idea to a prerequisite. If required factual material is absent, name the gap; do not invent supporting experience or evidence.

Keep the opening promise in view as the article grows. End when that promise has been fulfilled and the journey reaches a natural close, even with unused fragments. On a requested rewrite, edit the identified beat and flag any downstream conceptual dependency it changes. Never write ahead of the chosen beat.
