## Artificial Intelligence and the Upcoming Intelligence Explosion

At the dawn of the intelligence explosion, I find it extremely important to maintain situational awareness on the AI industry, AI policy, and the debates related to these topics. Over the last few years, my revealed preferences look like this:

- **Weekly**, I read [The Zvi Substack AI newsletter](https://thezvi.substack.com/). Zvi gets into the weeds and processes through the chaos of the AI Twitter Hivemind to come up with a high-bandwidth weekly "much more than you wanted to know" digest commentary.
- For more in-depth AI discussions, I listen to podcasts like Dwarkesh Podcast, 80,000 Hours Podcast, and sometimes the Cognitive Revolution Podcast — also, if you are selective, Lex Fridman Podcast. I find the AI Explained YouTube account extremely valuable and based.
- For books on the topic, I recommend *Superintelligence* by Nick Bostrom and *The Precipice* by Toby Ord.
- Also check out the following Twitter lists if you want to get into the weeds yourself and participate in the discussions: The Zvi AI Twitter lists and Alex K. Chen lists.

## Meditation Practice

Meditation practice is the highest ROI endeavor you could take as a conscious human being, speaking in terms of understanding experientially what is going on, understanding the nature of consciousness and the human condition at the intimate level of direct experience. Understanding and mastering your operating system thoroughly from the inside out has all kinds of life-changing implications. It's the best thing you could do for yourself in terms of dealing with the cards that nature/life dealt to you. It's the most important skill/trait to cultivate for dealing/relating to the specific human condition — the body/mind that you are. It is key to figuring out how to liberate from the many bonds/inflictions/traps that cause human suffering and alienation. It's one of the rare (grounded in nature of mind) ways to human flourishing and wellbeing. It's crucial not just for yourself but also for your loved ones as interdependent/interrelated conscious beings, and other sentient beings more broadly. If you are curious about the fundamental existential questions — What is this? Who are you? What is possible? What's going on? What is of value? What matters? Are we on the right track as a civilization/species? — please try to establish a serious meditation practice. Your sanity depends on it.

---

**TLDR — "ENOUGH EVANGELISM, LET ME START RIGHT NOW AND SEE FOR MYSELF":**

Go to the corner of your room and sit in silence for an hour. That's it. Just do it. No expectations, no instructions, no doing, no figuring out — just sit and curiously sense what happens.

Otherwise, guided meditations are the best way a beginner could start:

- Go to [Michael Taft's YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@MichaelTaftMeditation) and try out one of his guided meditations — he is a great guide and teacher.
- Or download the [Waking Up App](https://www.wakingup.com/) by Sam Harris, which is a hub for the best non-dual meditation teachers — highly recommended.
- Or if you want to immerse yourself in a daylong/weekend-long intensive, go to: Shinzen Young's Day-Long Retreat at the Monastic Academy (starts at 19m 20s). Shinzen is widely considered a rare, highly realized meditation master in the West.

### Recommended Books

Here are the books recommended by r/streamentry:

- *The Mind Illuminated* by Upasaka Culadasa. An abridged meditation guide (pdf) is freely available. Also see the dedicated subreddit [r/TheMindIlluminated](https://www.reddit.com/r/TheMindIlluminated/).
- For an alternative, freely available guide to the same style of meditation, see Thanissaro Bhikkhu's *With Each and Every Breath* (preview the main practice instructions).
- *Seeing That Frees* by Rob Burbea — a deep exploration of insight for the experienced practitioner. Rob's many outstanding audio talks are also freely available.
- *Mastering the Core Teachings of the Buddha* by Daniel Ingram ([free online](https://www.mctb.org/), also available in print). Also see the [Dharma Overground forums](https://www.dharmaoverground.org/).
- *Our Pristine Mind* by Orgyen Chowang Rinpoche — a pragmatic and secular guide to Dzogchen specifically written for Westerners. Enlightenment is clearly defined and the stages of awakening as understood in Dzogchen are thoroughly explained in plain language. Recommended for anyone interested in an "awareness-based" approach to Awakening.
- *Right Concentration* by Leigh Brasington — an accessible and comprehensive guide to jhāna practice. Jhāna (meditative absorption) is sometimes described as one of the two wings of awakening, the other being insight practice. See also the book's website, which contains supplementary material.

To understand theoretically what meditation is and gain practical instruction that will equip you well to seriously engage with this practice, read first — *MCTB 2*. I have read this book more than 5 times; it is in my case the most valuable resource on this really important existential topic. It also provides a nice overview of all the possibilities, different practices, and valuable books. If you want to go straight to these references, visit the [library of Daniel Ingram](https://www.mctb.org/mctb2/table-of-contents/part-vi-a-revised-buddhist-path/42-the-library/).

Here is a [great list of recommended books by Michael Taft](https://deconstructingyourself.com/resources).

For useful advice that will properly set your attitude:

- Read this guide from Buddhist Geeks.
- [Tasshin's meditation tips for a lifetime practitioner](https://tasshin.com/blog/meditation-tips/)
- See also his links: [Tasshin's useful meditation resources](https://tasshin.com/blog/resources/)

### Meditation Apps

- **[Waking Up](https://www.wakingup.com/)** by Sam Harris — for a non-dual meditation approach, this is an amazing resource. I recommend his introductory course. See also courses by other great non-dual teachers: Loch Kelly, Stephen Bodian, Adyashanti, Henry Shukman, and others. Sam also has longer meditations which are great if you want to go deeper.
- **Bright Mind App** — worse as an app (poor software implementation) but with great teachers: Shinzen Young, Vince and Emily Horn, Stephanie Nash, and many others. Really good stuff.

### Great Meditation Courses

- Culadasa's *Sit Breath Wake Up* on YouTube — for beginners who want to learn how to stabilize attention, with useful background theory.
- [Michael Taft's YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@MichaelTaftMeditation) — a great resource for a long guided video meditation journey.
- The best course on the jhānas: the jhāna retreat by the one and only Rob Burbea.
- A fascinating long course: *Mahamudra for the Modern World* by Reginald A. Ray — one of my favorite meditation teachers. Check all of his books and courses, including his Somatic Meditation course.
