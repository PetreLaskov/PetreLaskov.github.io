## Editorial Target

Aim for a curated atlas: part field guide, part reference work, part
hypertext book.

The atlas should feel like a map room:

- clear enough for humans to browse;
- precise enough to preserve Shinzen's practice distinctions;
- source-calibrated enough not to overclaim;
- safety-aware enough not to turn practice language into therapy, authority,
  or metaphysics.

Avoid both extremes:

- do not copy the internal wiki's dense AI-oriented memory pages;
- do not reduce the atlas to short explanatory stubs.

## Working Method

Use the internal compiler as the back office for judgment, not as the public
voice. For each substantial edit, begin with the reader's job, then mine the
relevant internal owner page for practice handles, source posture, safety
limits, contradictions, and high-value links. Bring forward the insight, not
the machinery.

Strong atlas pages should make the internal work feel effortless to the
reader: enough structure to choose the next move, enough source calibration
to trust the distinctions, and enough examples to make the language usable.

## Voice and Register

Write for serious practitioners and careful readers. The voice should be
precise, warm, plainspoken, source-calibrated, and practice-facing.

Avoid:

- generic wellness copy;
- guru-transmission language;
- academic distance that drains the practice handles;
- internal compiler language such as routing contracts, model deltas, claim
  IDs, or agent-facing instructions.

Prefer:

- concrete practice problems before abstract names;
- Shinzen-specific terms when they carry real discrimination;
- short claim-tier language such as "Shinzen says," "compiled synthesis,"
  "editorial inference," "speculative extension," and "not established
  here";
- page-specific safety cautions over repeated boilerplate;
- links that answer a reader's likely next question.

## Public Page Shape

The new atlas shape is tiered:

1. **Pillars** are the ten path chapters. They carry the living through-line,
   then point down into practice and boundary pages.
2. **Practice pages** are standalone lookup pages. They should open with the
   live move, stay operational, include concrete examples or decision tables
   when useful, carry only page-specific safety, and begin `Next Reading`
   with their path chapter.
3. **Boundary/reference pages** are where rigor concentrates: claim tiers,
   safety, guidance scope, source/citation posture, glossary entries, and
   full lookup.

Do not reintroduce the old fixed skeleton, repeated generic disclaimer
paragraph, or per-page `Source Posture` / `Source Trail` sections. Use tables
when they create a decision surface, not as decoration.
