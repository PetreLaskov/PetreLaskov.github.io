# Practice Report Check

A useful practice report is not the most dramatic story about what happened. It is the report that makes the next wise move easier to see.

Shinzen's system can route very different situations through similar words. "No self," "thoughts are loud," "pain opened," "nothing happened," "I got calm," "I fell into the void," or "practice made me kinder" are not enough by themselves. The same phrase can point to a method adjustment, a safety boundary, a behavior check, a support need, or a simple instruction to keep going.

Use this page when you are writing notes, asking a teacher, checking the atlas, or trying to decide whether to stay, tune, switch, turn away, stop, or get support.

## The Six-Part Report

| Report layer | Ask | Why it routes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Context | What method, range, posture, setting, and life situation were active? | The same event means different things in Noting, Do Nothing, Rest, Focus Out, or daily life. |
| Sensory facts | What was directly known as See, Hear, Feel, Image, Talk, Rest, Flow, Gone, or Space? | Shinzen's methods work on trackable sensory events, not only on interpretations. |
| CCE quality | Was there more concentration, sensory clarity, and equanimity, or less? | The route is suspect if it makes experience vague, frantic, numb, or compulsive. |
| Reaction | What craving, fear, pride, disappointment, confusion, or fascination appeared afterward? | The reaction to a practice effect may be the next object, if it is workable. |
| Life effect | What changed in behavior, repair, speech, work, relationship, support, or service? | Practice is not self-validating if conduct and responsiveness are not improving. |
| Boundary | What ordinary care, safety, consent, sleep, medical, clinical, teacher, or accountability issue belongs in the frame? | Some reports should slow technique down rather than intensify it. |

If a report cannot answer all six layers, that does not make it bad. It simply means the next move should stay modest.

The better report does not need to be long. It needs enough context to prevent the wrong advice from sounding plausible.

## Stay, Tune, Switch, Stop

After one session, one day, or one realistic practice window, ask:

| Next move | Report signal |
| --- | --- |
| Stay | contact is clearer, struggle is lower, and life remains responsive. |
| Tune | the route is basically right, but one control is off: range, label pace, effort, scope, posture, dose, or sequence. |
| Switch | the method is solving the wrong problem, such as using Do Nothing for fog when labels would restore clarity. |
| Turn away or support | direct contact is too much, but the challenge can remain permitted in the background. |
| Stop optimizing | safety, sleep, behavior, medical care, trauma support, teacher pressure, or ordinary protection is now the main issue. |

This is the branch-and-loop shape: name what happened, choose a provisional route, check the result, and update. Do not turn a single session report into a permanent self-diagnosis.

## What Not To Infer

A strong sensation is not proof of purification. A calm state is not proof of equanimity. A no-self report is not proof of healthy function. A Source afterglow is not proof of metaphysics, ethics, science, or teacher authority. A helpful instruction from someone does not prove they have scope for every part of your life.

Also be careful with anti-reporting. "I should not think about practice" can be useful when analysis is obsessive, but it can also hide avoidable confusion. A short, sensory, behavior-aware report often reduces rumination because it gives the next move a smaller shape.

## When To Ask For Help

Ask for competent help sooner when practice is worsening, frightening, isolating, linked to trauma or medical issues, reducing sleep or function, increasing harmful behavior, making teacher pressure harder to question, or making ordinary care seem less important.

The best report for a helper is honest and bounded: what you practiced, what happened in sensory terms, what changed in CCE, what happened in life, and what support or safety question is active.

Use [Asking for Guidance](asking-for-guidance.md) when the next step is an actual conversation with a teacher, coach, peer, clinician, recovery sponsor, friend, or other helper. The report prepares the facts; that page prepares the ask, the role boundary, and the follow-up loop.
