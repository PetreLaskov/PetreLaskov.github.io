# Windows and Walls

A Window is an opening. A Wall is an obstacle. Shinzen's useful move is to treat both as practice-routing moments without pretending that either one is automatically safe, meaningful, or spiritually good.

Sometimes life gives practice a natural opening: energy, interest, grief that wants to be felt, love that wants to become action, a clear chance to repair, or a state that makes practice unusually available. Sometimes life gives the opposite: illness, injury, betrayal, violence, loss, shame, conflict, or the consequences of one's own harmful behavior. Shinzen's "Windows and Walls" language says: if practice is appropriate, name what is actually here and choose the next branch.

The hard boundary is just as important as the opportunity. A catastrophe is not made good by becoming practice material. Ordinary care, protection, repair, medical support, therapy, legal response, rest, and accountability may need to lead before any meditation route is useful.

## The Four Things To Name

Before turning a Wall or Window into technique, name four layers.

| Layer | Question | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Objective situation | What is actually happening in life? | Meditation should not replace facts, safety, role duties, or ordinary strategy. |
| Objective behavior | What needs to change, stop, repair, or continue? | A practice insight is not complete if behavior and commitments disappear. |
| Sensory challenge | What is the See, Hear, Feel, Image, Talk, Rest, Flow, or Gone material? | This is where Shinzen's methods become precise instead of vague. |
| Support and feedback | Who can help, correct, protect, or check the process? | High-intensity practice is more trustworthy when it is not isolated or self-certifying. |

This is the public version of the guidance loop: understand the situation, choose a branch, see what changes, and revise. It is not a diagnosis and not a crisis protocol.

## Route The Wall Or Window

| What is live | First route | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A clear opening appears: interest, gratitude, love, positive energy, available practice time, or a workable insight window. | Use one simple method or a planned sequence. Let the opening become steadiness, clarity, positive reconstruction, or service. | Chasing the opening, inflating it, or using it to skip behavior and repair. |
| A challenge is intense but workable. | Name the sensory challenge, then choose [turn toward, turn away, Flow, Rest, or support](turn-toward-and-turn-away.md). | Making direct contact heroic, or making turn-away into disappearance-seeking. |
| A life event is severe: illness, injury, betrayal, grief, violence, crisis, abuse, coercion, or major loss. | Start with [Safety, Scope, and Accountability](safety-scope-and-accountability.md) and [Applied Life Boundaries](applied-life-boundaries.md); for serious illness or a diagnosis, [Serious Illness and Healing](serious-illness-and-healing.md). Practice may be secondary. | Romanticizing trauma, minimizing danger, or treating "life as monastery" as a command to endure. |
| The Wall is one's own behavior: addiction, harm, avoidance, conflict, or repeated failure to repair. | Decompose urges if workable, then add behavior commitments, repair, feedback, and outside accountability. | Mistaking insight into the urge for actual behavior change. |
| A teacher, group, or helper wants to route a Wall or Window for you. | Check consent, competence, scope, feedback, and dependency before trusting the guidance. | Spiritual pressure, secrecy, shame, status, or lack of ordinary accountability. |
| The situation is too confusing to route. | Simplify practice or stop optimizing meditation first. Handle sleep, food, safety, support, and ordinary obligations. | Treating confusion as proof that a more advanced technique is needed. |

## One Method, Workout, Or Branch

Shinzen's system can be used at three scales.

One method reduces choice. If the Wall or Window is already overwhelming, one clear route may be better than the full menu.

A workout sequence trains several capacities: ordinary sensory contact, Rest, Flow or Gone, Do Nothing, and positive reconstruction. It can be useful when the system needs both deconstruction and rebuilding.

Looping and branching is the flexible mode. The route changes when interest, opportunity, or necessity changes. This is powerful, but it has a complexity cost. Without enough CCE and feedback, it can become constant self-optimization.

In Shinzen's teaching, a trained facilitator may have a larger branch library than the practitioner can safely run alone. Publicly, read that modestly: strong life Walls and Windows are places to include competent support, not places to prove solitary toughness.

## How It Goes Wrong

The most common mistake is spiritualizing the event too soon. "This can be practice" is different from "this should have happened" or "I should stay inside it." Practice language should never make harm, neglect, coercion, or preventable suffering harder to see.

Another mistake is premature resurrection. Focus on Positive, service, forgiveness, or meaning can be healthy after a Wall, but not if it skips grief, protection, accountability, medical care, or repair.

The algorithm metaphor can also overpromise. A route map can help a practitioner move intelligently; it cannot guarantee safety, permanent growth, or the right answer in a crisis.
