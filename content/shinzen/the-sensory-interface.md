# The Sensory Interface

Shinzen turns "be mindful" into coordinates. See / Hear / Feel is not a claim about what reality ultimately is. It is a way to make present experience findable, trackable, and workable, so that "pay attention" becomes an instruction you can actually carry out.

The older **Sensory Grid** names channels and modes. See, Hear, and Feel cross with In, Out, Rest, and Flow, plus Gone. Mental image is See In; mental talk is Hear In; emotional body sensation is Feel In. Physical sight is See Out, sound is Hear Out, body sensation is Feel Out. Rest is the stillness, quiet, blank, or peace in a channel. Flow is the movement, vibration, pressure, spread, or collapse in it. Gone is the noticed vanishing.

The later Unified Mindfulness interface compresses the grid into three labels and a focus range. That is elegant and easy to teach, but the range has to stay visible. See can mean mental image in Focus In, physical sight in Focus Out, visual stillness in Focus on Rest, or visual change in a Flow practice. The word does not carry the whole instruction by itself. Range plus label points the mind.

This is why the grid is a routing grammar, not an ontology. Experience does not arrive pre-cut into Shinzen's boxes. The boxes are useful precisely because a practitioner can pick one up: attention can choose a single cell, a column, a row, or the whole field. They turn a fog of "I feel terrible" into a workable pattern - tight Feel in the chest, looping Talk, flashes of Image, the background Rest gone missing, Flow available at the edge.

**Feel / Image / Talk** is the most load-bearing slice, because so much of what we call self, emotion, meaning, memory, anxiety, and reactivity is built from these three strands tangling faster than awareness can tell them apart. An anxiety, looked at closely, is often a knot of chest Feel, a catastrophe Image, and one sentence of Talk on a loop. Naming the three does not banish the anxiety; it slackens the knot enough that each strand can be met on its own.

None of this is meant to make practice fussy. It is meant to make the next contact possible. Sometimes the exact label is obvious; sometimes a rough guess is fine; sometimes plain "Feel" is enough; sometimes dropping the label and staying with the object is cleaner. The Four Okays around noting exist because precision without mercy becomes its own trap.

## Finding A Handle

When experience is too tangled to work and you cannot find where to put attention, name the felt state on the left and start from the coordinate on the right.

| When experience is... | Useful coordinate |
| --- | --- |
| emotional or self-heavy | Feel / Image / Talk |
| scattered by thought | Hear In and See In |
| pain or body intensity | Feel Out, and Feel Flow |
| calm, blank, quiet, or restful | Rest |
| vibrating, pulsing, spreading | Flow |
| ending, dropping, disappearing | Gone |
| too complex to label exactly | a broader See / Hear / Feel with a clear range |
