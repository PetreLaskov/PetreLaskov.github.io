# Practice Planning Loop

A practice plan is not a promise to conquer the atlas. It is a small loop:
choose an aim, choose a container, practice, read the report, adjust, and
check life.

Use this page when you want the next week or month to become actual practice
without overchoice, status comparison, or unsafe intensity. The point is not
to touch every method. The point is to keep one honest loop alive long enough
for feedback to mean something.

## The Loop

| Step | Ask | Keep it modest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Name the aim | What is the live job: reduce suffering, elevate fulfillment, understand self, change behavior, cultivate love, or support service? | One aim can lead the cycle while the others stay visible. |
| Choose the container | Is this a one-technique period, a planned workout, responsive branching, or a support-first situation? | The container protects practice from constant shopping. |
| Set the cycle | What belongs this week: stillness, motion, micro-hits, daily-life carryover, feedback, rest, or fun? | A small stable rhythm beats a heroic plan that collapses. |
| Practice | What method, range, posture, and dose are actually being used? | Make the experiment bounded enough to review. |
| Read the report | What happened in context, sensory facts, CCE quality, reaction, life effect, and boundary? | Use [Practice Report Check](practice-report-check.md) when the story is too vague. |
| Adjust | Should you stay, tune, switch, turn away, get support, or stop optimizing? | Adjust one control before rebuilding the whole path. |
| Check life | Is behavior, repair, relationship, support, service, or ordinary functioning improving? | Private experience does not self-certify the plan. |

The loop can be very small. A useful week might be ten minutes of stillness,
one short walk, three low-stakes micro-hits, one honest report, and one
behavior check. The plan has succeeded if it produces clearer contact and
better responsiveness, even if no dramatic state appears.

## One-Week Starter Loops

| Live problem | Starter loop | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Too many methods | Pick one route for a week; write two brief reports; tune effort or range before switching. | Turning route choice into identity or technique collecting. |
| Pain, emotion, or pressure is present | Decide whether to turn toward, turn away with background permission, focus on Flow, or use support first. | Treating intensity as proof that the practice is working. |
| Daily life does not carry over | Use one formal sit, one formal-motion period, and a few low-demand micro-hits. Practice afterward for high-stakes tasks. | Dividing attention when ordinary task contact should lead. |
| A behavior pattern keeps repeating | Practice with the sensory urge if workable, then add a concrete behavior commitment or accountability check. | Imagining that insight alone has handled the consequence. |
| A deep or open state appeared | Use [Retreat and Aftercare](retreat-and-aftercare.md); keep the next cycle simple: sleep, food, a normal sit, light micro-hits, feedback, and behavior checks. | Chasing afterglow, dismissing aftershock, or overreading what the state proves. |
| Practice is becoming harsh | Reduce dose, soften effort, use Rest or background equanimity, and check safety/support. | Calling strain discipline or shutdown equanimity. |

## Month Rhythm

A month does not need a complicated curriculum. It needs enough repetition to
see what the method is doing.

1. Week one: choose the aim and container.
2. Week two: keep the container and tune one obvious control.
3. Week three: test carryover into motion, micro-hits, or a simple life
   challenge.
4. Week four: review practice reports and life effects before adding or
   changing a method.

The review question is not "Did I advance?" A better question is: "What is
clearer, less binding, more kind, more functional, or more honestly in need
of support?"

## What Not To Optimize

Do not optimize for page coverage. Reading every page is not a practice plan.

Do not optimize for the most advanced-sounding object. Flow, Gone, no-self,
Source language, and service language all need ordinary checks.

Do not optimize for harder intensity. Workable CCE matters more than heroic
endurance.

Do not optimize for continuous technique. Some tasks, conversations, safety
roles, and care duties need ordinary attention first.

Do not optimize for private proof. The plan is tested by practice quality,
behavior, support, humility, repair, and service.
