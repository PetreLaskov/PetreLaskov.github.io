# Noting

When a thought or a pain or a wave of emotion has you, the trouble is rarely the content. It is that you are fused with it, carried off, with nothing to take hold of. Noting is the handle. You acknowledge a sensory event and rest attention on it for a moment, and that small act of naming-and-touching grows concentration, sensory clarity, and equanimity at once.

The essence is not the spoken word. It is the acknowledging and the focusing. The label can be spoken or mental, strong or soft, fast or slow, repeated, or dropped the instant it stops helping. A single note can break the spell of identification: "anger" resolves into Feel, Image, Talk; "the room" into See, Hear, Feel; "nothing is happening" turns out to be Rest, Gone, or plain drifting.

In its simplest form:

1. Notice a sensory event.
2. Name it with a useful label: See, Hear, Feel, Rest, Flow, Gone.
3. Focus on it briefly.
4. Let the next event appear.

The aim is to strengthen contact, not to produce perfect labels. So the label flexes with what the moment needs: a mental note when practice is stable, a spoken one when attention keeps vanishing, a strong one when the sit is a steep climb, none at all when contact is already clear. Repeat a note while its event is still live; note Gone when something drops away.

## First Minute

For a first trial, keep the container small.

| Moment | Practice move | Keep out of it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before starting | Choose a range: outer sound and body, inner talk and emotion, Rest, or Everything. | Covering the whole system at once, unless Everything is the chosen range. |
| An event appears | Name it with the simplest useful label, then focus for one to three seconds. | Analyzing the story, hunting a better label, scoring the sit. |
| Several at once | Choose one, or use an inclusive label only when that genuinely helps contact. | Turning simultaneity into a precision exam. |
| The same event stays | Repeat the note, soften it, or stay until part of the event drops away. | Demanding a fresh object just to feel progress. |
| Attention disappears | Use a stronger or spoken label for a while, then ease off once contact holds. | Treating the stronger label as failure. |

## Small Examples

If mental talk keeps taking over, the note may be Talk, Hear In, or just Hear, depending on the range you are working. If your range is outer sight and sound, that same talk can be left in the background with equanimity instead of pulled to the front.

If a body sensation is intense but workable, note Feel or Touch, hold it a few seconds, zoom to a smaller region, or repeat the note until part of it drops away and Gone becomes available.

If "nothing is happening," noting tends to find something anyway: rest, a faint image, subtle talk, the body going quiet, the disappearance of whatever was just here. You never have to invent content.

## Where Noting Curdles

Two ways, both worth catching early. The first is commentary: when the label starts generating more thinking about the event than contact with it, the note has become a story about the experience. Simplify it, or drop it and sense directly. The second is harsher - when the labeling itself starts to hurt. Notes that turn fast and shaming, labeling so you do not have to feel, or running at a teacher's or retreat's pace because you think you should, have stopped serving contact and started replacing it. That is the signal to soften or stop, not to label harder.

And anywhere attention is load-bearing - driving, machinery, childcare, a hard conversation, care work - noting is not a second channel running over the task. There it collapses back to plain sensory contact with the task itself.
