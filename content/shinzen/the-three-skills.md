# The Three Skills

Mindfulness comes apart. What looks like a single blurry virtue is, in Shinzen's system, three trainable skills working together: **concentration power**, **sensory clarity**, and **equanimity**. The shorthand is CCE, but the acronym is not the useful part. The useful part is that each skill has a taste, each can be trained on its own, and any one of them can be missing while the other two look strong.

**Concentration power** is the ability to stay with what matters. It is not clenching the mind around an object and treating the rest of experience as the enemy. Healthy concentration selects what is relevant and returns without war. It tastes like gatheredness, continuity, being in the zone: the quiet reward of attention that has stopped scattering at every touch.

**Sensory clarity** is the ability to know what is actually here. It unmakes the blur. A mood resolves into body Feel, mental Image, and mental Talk. A pain resolves into pressure, heat, pulse, spread, edge, center. A self resolves into strands. The vague becomes trackable, and what is trackable becomes workable. This is the skill that keeps mindfulness from collapsing into calm attentiveness that sees very little.

**Equanimity** is non-interference. It is not numbness, niceness, passivity, or approval. It is the third possibility between suppression and capture: not pushing the event down, not drowning in it, not setting the body's circuits against their own productions. Equanimity lets experience do what experience does, which is arise, change, vanish, and return.

The tastes matter because they let you diagnose practice from the inside. Concentration tastes like collectedness. Clarity tastes like resolution, the blur breaking into parts. Equanimity tastes like the relief of allowing a present event so that past material loosens and the next moment binds less. Shinzen points to that relief as the taste of purification: not the drama of pain, but non-interference quietly doing its work.

Most plateaus are not failures of effort; they are skill imbalances. Concentration without clarity becomes pleasant absorption that does not see much. Clarity without equanimity becomes sharp analysis that opens the system without softening it. Equanimity without clarity becomes smooth blankness, the good-place trap. So the question is rarely "am I being mindful?" It is "which axis is low right now?"

There is one especially kind implication of treating these as separate skills. When equanimity is the one that has gone missing, do not turn equanimity into one more demand. Be equanimous with not being equanimous. Non-interference with your own interference is still the training, and it is often the way back in.

## Which Axis Is Low

When a sit is off and you cannot feel which skill is starving it, read the symptom on the left and check the axis on the right before changing anything else.

| If practice feels... | Check first |
| --- | --- |
| scattered, jumpy, unable to stay | concentration power |
| vague, global, hard to locate | sensory clarity |
| tight, braced, flooded, suppressive | equanimity |
| calm but dull | sensory clarity, and plain alertness |
| precise but harsh | equanimity inside the clarity |
| strong but exhausting | equanimity inside the concentration |
