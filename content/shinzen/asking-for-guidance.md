# Asking for Guidance

Good guidance is a loop, not a surrender of judgment. Bring a bounded report, ask for a practice hypothesis, and keep consent, ordinary care, and feedback in the room.

Use this page before talking with a meditation teacher, coach, peer, therapist, physician, recovery sponsor, friend, or community helper about practice. It does not tell you whom to trust. It helps make the conversation specific enough that the helper's role and limits can be seen.

## The Basic Move

1. Make the report in your own terms.
2. Ask what practice hypothesis fits the report.
3. Clarify the helper's role and limits.
4. Try only a bounded experiment.
5. Check what happened in practice, behavior, support, and safety.

This is the same branch-and-loop shape used throughout the atlas: report, route, test, revise. The helper may see a useful branch you missed, but the result still has to show up in your actual practice and life.

## Before You Ask

Bring a short report, not your whole identity as a practitioner.

| Bring | Useful form |
| --- | --- |
| Context | "I was doing Noting for twenty minutes, mostly Feel and Talk, before sleep." |
| Sensory facts | "The main event was chest pressure, fast Talk, and brief waves of Flow." |
| CCE quality | "Clarity improved, but equanimity dropped and labels became harsh." |
| Reaction | "Afterward I felt proud, then worried I was forcing purification." |
| Life effect | "Sleep was worse and I was sharper with my partner the next morning." |
| Boundary | "I want practice advice, but sleep and relationship repair have to stay in the frame." |

If you cannot fill all six parts, say that. A partial report is better than an impressive story that hides the routing facts.

## Ask For A Hypothesis

The strongest question is not "what does this mean about me?" It is:

> What practice hypothesis fits this report, and what would make you change it?

Useful follow-up questions:

- "Should I stay with this method, tune one control, switch methods, turn away for support, pause, or get outside help?"
- "What should I watch for during the next session?"
- "What would count as a good result besides a dramatic state?"
- "What should I stop doing if this worsens?"
- "What part of this is outside your role?"
- "When should I ask someone else or seek qualified care?"

Ask for enough explanation that you can practice more intelligently. You do not need a cosmic verdict, a permanent stage diagnosis, or a total interpretation of your life.

## What To Share And Withhold

Share what changes the route: method, range, dose, context, sensory facts, CCE quality, reaction, behavior, sleep, support, safety concerns, and any ordinary-life constraints that make the advice safer or less safe.

You do not owe a helper unlimited private detail. Trauma, sexuality, medical history, family conflict, money, legal risk, or organizational harm should be shared only as far as the role, consent, competence, and safety of the conversation justify. If those details govern the situation, the next move may be a qualified helper rather than more meditation instruction.

Do not hide immediate safety facts to protect a practice story. Self-harm risk, harm to others, abuse, coercion, medical danger, severe dissociation, loss of function, unsafe sleep deprivation, or teacher misconduct should move the conversation toward ordinary support and protection first.

## Good Signs And Warning Signs

| Good sign | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| The helper asks what actually happened before advising. | The answer stays tied to your report. |
| They explain why a branch fits and what would change the branch. | Guidance stays testable instead of becoming a pronouncement. |
| You are free to accept, modify, decline, ask why, or seek another view. | Practice remains consent-based. |
| The advice includes a follow-up check. | The loop can learn from results. |
| They name role limits and referral points. | Meditation does not absorb therapy, medicine, recovery, legal protection, or emergency care. |
| You become clearer, more responsive, and less dependent over time. | Good help should increase independence. |

| Warning sign | Why to slow down |
| --- | --- |
| The helper gives global pronouncements from a thin report. | Stage, Source, karma, trauma, or destiny language can outrun evidence. |
| Private disclosure is pressured beyond the role. | Intimacy is not consent, and guidance is not entitlement. |
| Doubt, refusal, second opinions, or outside care are discouraged. | Dependence is replacing practice judgment. |
| Secrecy, retaliation, special access, money, sexuality, or loyalty pressure enters the frame. | Ordinary accountability has become the main issue. |
| Harmful behavior is spiritualized away. | Realization language is failing the behavior test. |
| The advice keeps worsening practice, sleep, function, or relationships. | The loop is giving a stop or support signal. |

## After The Advice

Treat the advice as a small experiment unless the situation is clearly about ordinary safety or care, in which case support comes first.

For the next practice window, write down:

- what you tried;
- what changed in concentration, sensory clarity, and equanimity;
- what happened afterward in behavior, sleep, work, relationships, or support;
- what boundary or referral question remains.

Then ask: did this make practice more workable, or did it make the problem bigger? If the result worsens, becomes compulsive, reduces function, or makes ordinary care feel less important, slow down and route through safety, scope, and qualified support.
